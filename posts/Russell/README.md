# Russell

A while back I picked up a volume of Russell's autobiography at the Tacoma Book Center.  I'd known of Russell primarily for his (and Whitehead's) failed and impossible task to prove mathematics in the Principia.  Later Logicomix came out, highlighting his pacifist beliefs as well.  I'd read, and enjoyed, Ghandi's memoir, so though maybe I'd like this.

It had been slow going -- a tale of a ship crossing guarded by American destroyers in WWII.  I started flipping through a bit.  I came to a part on the Cuban Missile Crisis.  In it he characterized the Americans as unwilling to let the Cubans self govern, completely overlooking that whole "Soviet nuclear missiles 70 miles off the coast of Florida" bit.  So, ok, that's a bit disingenous.

Then later he shares a letter to Kruschev asking for nuclear disarmerment, one leftist to another.  The profound naivete of that letter was a bit shocking to me.  At this point he must be 60 or more and not at all wise.

I suppose I shouldn't be shocked.   The brilliant mathematicians I've known have never had much walking around sense.  why should one of the most ambitious mathematicians of the 20th century be any different?  The whole thing feels timely with populism and Communism on the rise again.  I imagine the Soviets viewed Russell as a "useful idiot."

If you want a mathematical memoir with a bit more insight, I'd suggest "A Mathematician's Apology."
