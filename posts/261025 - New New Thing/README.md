# 261025 - New New Thing

...Written from Heathrow during a layover...

I think the first Michael Lewis book I read was The New New Thing.  I read it in The Trident in one sitting, chuckling to myself.  At one point a guy came up and said "that's a good book."  I thought he'd read it, but later realized he meant I was enjoying it.

The book is about Jim Clark of SGI fame.  I remember his fancy sailboat automated with SGIs goes haywire.  Lewis said that was him at his happiest, debugging that thing.

I'm wondering what my new new thing is.  

## Bayside Trails

A few years back I went deep down the rabbit hole of [reopening a hiking trail](https://benofben.github.io/baysidetrails/).  I still cannot quite believe the resistence.  Who doesn't like a hiking trail?  It turns out lots of people...

Ultimately that led me to run for city council.  I have the odd distinction of having been attacked by the government, in this case Parks Tacoma.  That ultimately led to a [formal apology](https://www.reddit.com/r/TacomaPolitics/comments/1xcpv4e/parks_tacoma_apology_to_ben_lackey/).

I fought city hall and won.  I think I did a good thing for my neighbors, myself and my kids.  There's more to be done, but I think I'm likely past a point of diminishing returns.  There are likely bigger (and newer, more interesting) fish to fry than reforming the parks department.  With any luck that's already on its way regardless, hopefully with a good shove in the right direction from me.  

And, I should note, that while only 1/3 of the trail is reopened, that 1/3 is all that I was aware of in 2022.  If someone could describe the current state to 2022 me, that person would probably be pleasantly surprised at the outcome -- a restored trail, passable by our kids and an apology for any nastiness along the way.

I'm happy my name is cleared and the paper of record (for Tacoma) has [documented it as such](https://www.thenewstribune.com/news/local/article317720825.html).  The vindication is really similar to what I've felt when a large software deal I worked on significantly closes.  Funny enough, the subsequent feeling of "what next?" is also very similar.

## JRUCS

Over the last year I went pretty deep on another project, [JRUCS](https://github.com/jrucs).  That's another I'm quite proud of.  It's a trading card game.  I think the design is good.  I think it's an innovative use of AI.  And I learned a ton making it.  That said, there is no product market fit for reasons I've [discussed previously](https://benlackey.com/Proxy).

I think it's probably time to unwind the Amazon seller account, Adobe subcription and such that are burning $100/month or so.  I'm not quite sure what to do with the 10s of thousands of cards in the basement.  And I kinda want to finish the thing I started.  Fire, Water and Air are done.  Earth is maybe 1/4 done.

I'm a little tempted to go ahead and make it, as an ode to geology and dinosaurs.  My thought is Olive would quite enjoy it.  I think that's the big thing keeping me from total unwind even though it's on my to do list.

One thing I learned from making it is how vehement the anti AI people are.  As a wise man once said, the future is not evenly distributed.  I really think changes are here and more are coming.  Firmly inserting your head in the sand is not a solution.  Though, to paraphrase Keynes, I suspect a society can remain irrational longer than you can remain alive.

I'm reading a book called "The Wealth and Poverty of Nations."  One subject in it is the collapse of empires.  Portugal and Spain were particularly painful to read about.  Their collapse took hundreds of years.  Arguably it continues today, 500 years after the high point.  The solution was not to outwite the inquisition, rather to get the hell away from it.  Sometimes the grass really is greener.

# A brief history of flight

As JRUCS has been winding down I used Gemini to make a [kids book on flight](https://github.com/benofben/a-brief-history-of-flight).  I think it is good.  Olive has certainly enjoyed it, though perhaps partly because he enjoys looking at the picture of us I stuck on the back page.

I don't particularly believe it will be a best seller.  Though, it has sold a handful of copies.  I'm sure there will be some tax form that the accountant charges more to incorporate into my return than I recieved in royalties.

It's a good project.  But I don't think it's the new new thing.

## What got you here

There's a book I like called "What got you here won't get you there."  Like many business books the title, and perhaps the first 10 pages, get the idea across.  The rest is just repetition.

A once good friend, now more distant and previous manager of mine made a comment to me.  It was something to the effect of "if people were robots, your life would be simple."  He was poking at me for expecting people to behave like the rational actors in economics textbooks, without accounting for emotion.  He was right.  And I'm still not where I'd like to be there.

I think my reports have typically enjoyed working for me.  I believe the teams I've led are very high performance, though I imagine everyone says that Garrison Keillor style.  I do have some record of quota overachievement most any time I carried one to point back at.  So, there is some data to support what I'd like to believe.

## What do you want to be when you grow up?

Of course, to get there I probably need to know where I'm going.

At this point, I'm late enough career that my network, both professional and friendly contains people that have done exceptionally well.  A handful run groups responsible for thousands of people and billions in revenue.  

I don't think I envy them.  And, it's not just because of the decades of long hours it took to get there.  I don't think I'd trade my varied experiences, piles of books read, trails walked and strange things built.  Heinlein says "specialization is for insects."  I think that might be extreme.  But I seem to suffer from a similar ailment to whatever he did.  I enjoy dabbling and changes of scenery.  That's probably why sales roles and startups have been so much fun.

I've had a suspicion for about a decade that the rich days of software sales are on their way out.  I think we've seen that with stagnant or even dropping sales and presales wages.  The channel has been an interesting place to hide from that.  As software sales commoditizes, things that let it scale have become more valuable.

One thing that was very strange about dabbling in politics is the need to win a majority over.  That's very different from sales, where the goal is to segment off a devoted customer base you can bring value to.

I go between disciplines a lot.

Technical sales, whether direct or channel, is probably where I've been most successful.

Investing has gone quite well, though there was the disasterous house remodel blip.  As Buffett has pointed out, it doesn't really matter what your returns are if you get a 0 one year.  That's basically what I did.  Painful, particularly because it wasn't even an investment error.  The asset was sound.  It was just poor management on my part.  Too much trust, not enough verify.  ...at least until it was too late.

Engineering can be fun but quickly gets repetitive.

And beyond that there would be some hobby.  But those have a lack of depth.  They go stale.  I suspect that's why you have people trying to make a business of painting warhammer figurines and such.  It's not as much they want their hobby to generate money, though that no doubt plays some role, rather they want it to generate meaning. And, I suspect hobbies struggle to do that.

Meaning, I think, comes from enaaging with othes.  That's why the hedge fund moguls are never content with yatchs, instead they start building research boats of funding schools.  Philantrophy is, like so much else, an attempt to extract meaning from the void.

## Paring things off

Every few years I go through one of these paring exercises.  I think I'm due for another.  I'm already pretty sure the AI art projects are falling off.

I'm tempted to drop properties off but I suspect I should stick with it a bit longer given interest rates and likely marco dynamics, at least if you're a Dalio or Marks believer.

I already went through the garage, trying to dump any tools that hadn't seen recent use or were one time crazy house repair things I hope never to do again.  More of that could probably be done.

I keep wondering about the large house.  I'm so torn on it.  It has enough room for everyone.  But boy is it both expensive and time consuming.  My current thought is that at some point, likely high school for the boys, we depart to Boulder.  That would allow them to take college classes in high school.  Though, who knows what college will look like then.

It's all well and good to have 5-10 year plans.  They just don't seem to hold up against reality all too well -- the current situation being prime evidence of that.

I'm just not certain I should stare at my computer even more.  At some point, I assume it is prudent to ramp that down and spend more time with people and nature.

Though, things like Gates' story of programming in his mind on a long hike do ressonate.
