# Parks Tacoma Apology to Ben Lackey

I've had a very strange experience the past few years. In early 2022 I bought a house in Stadium. We moved from 37th and Bennett. The new house was near Garfield Park. Way back in 2015 I'd noticed a trail below Garfield Park down through Garfield Gulch. It was abandoned and severely overgrown. Passing through the trail meant climbing on logs, through brush and wading through mud.

I began to remove things blocking the trail, including fallen logs, brush and a ridiculous amount of garbage. Memorably there was an entire toilet down there. I carried it out and hauled it to the dump. I removed tens of truckloads of garbage.

On a personal note, my dad’s health was failing. The backbreaking work was a distraction from a problem I couldn’t fix. I threw myself into it, relishing the ability to get so tired I could no longer ruminate, dreaming a dreamless sleep. My wife commented on the change in my physique as I grew muscles in places I’d never had them before.

As I was doing this work, I read up on Garfield Gulch. I came to understand that the trail I was reopening was part of the larger Bayside Trails system built in 1975. That trail system had been abandoned by the city and turned into a giant homeless camp.

Once I got the trail into a passable state, I reached out to Parks Tacoma. My thought was that we could improve the trail I’d reopened and reopen the remaining two thirds that were still closed. I was excited to partner with Parks to organize formal work parties.

Initially things seemed to go well. Mike Yaden, the Parks director, walked the trail with me multiple times and complimented my work. We discussed the best way to remove a tree stump.  He told a story about drinking on the Bayside Trail as a high schooler.

We began to organize "CHIP-in!" parties. I handed out flyers and doorbelled to drive attendance to those. As that was going on, Parks was working to renew a contract with the non profit Washington Trails Association (WTA) they had let lapse.  WTA agreed to host volunteer work parties in exchange for $120k of the public’s money. I thought that was strange as I was volunteering my labor for free at these WTA parties and did not understand why Parks would pay for something that they seemed to be able to accomplish for free. I now understand Parks believed the partnership was worth the tax dollars as WTA manages the organization and liability for Parks. Nonetheless, I spoke up against that, imagining a more efficient use of tax dollars.

The last Parks Tacoma work party I went to was attended by two people, me and one other guy who had come to previous ones.  He’d seen one of my flyers previously. We were told by a Parks employee to hack into the railroad grade, a stable grade that has existed since the 1890s.  I started to do it. Then I questioned myself and spoke up. I said something to the effect of "this is wrong, we shouldn't be doing this." The Parks employee took offense to my comment. I set down the McLeod trail tool I'd been using and walked away.

After that, Mike Yaden stopped responding to my calls or emails. A few weeks later I got a letter from Parks stating that I was banned from Parks’ properties for one year. I was surprised and hurt as I had spent hundreds of hours reopening the trail.  

Today, I have reviewed other Parks’ trespass orders and found that Parks rarely trespasses citizens. When they do, it involved things like sexual harassment, physically assaulting Parks’ employees to the point where the police were called, and walking around community centers completely unclothed while uttering profanities to Parks staff. Conversely, I felt Parks was using their trespass powers to attack me personally for criticizing them. The trespass letter contained legal errors that were obvious, even to me, a nonlawyer. For instance, it alleged I violated a city code that had been repealed and claimed that I had violated Parks’ social media policy that applies to Parks’ employees, not Parks’ volunteers or citizens who do not work for Parks such as myself. Parks has no ability to control what I want to say on social media and yet they decided to trespass me for doing just that.

I disagreed with the trespass letter and the accusations made against me, but I was away for the summer on paternity leave. Given that I was away, I just let the issue sit figuring it would age into irrelevance.  

While away, I wrote a website documenting the history of the Bayside Trails.  That is available [here](https://benofben.github.io/baysidetrails/).

I returned in the fall. I decided to enter the city council race with the focus being reopening the Bayside Trails. As Election Day neared, it appears a Park employee who held a grudge against me tipped the the Tacoma News Tribune (TNT) off about the trespass letter in hopes that it would damage my reputation and candidacy. I believe this is a violation of the 1st Amendment as it is a government employee improperly using their power to suppress my right to criticize the government. The TNT wrote an article about the trespass letter, which they are free to do. I was frustrated the article did not explain the entire story. 

In reviewing the trespass letter with an attorney, it was apparent that Parks issued the letter without any consultation of their attorney. As noted above, the letter cited laws that were no longer in effect, claimed I broke a social media policy only meant to apply to Parks employees, and failed to advise me of my right to appeal the letter to the Tacoma hearing examiner, which is a requirement under Tacoma law. In short, the trespass letter was legally flawed. 

While the one year trespass period had already lapsed, I still felt this letter was improperly causing me harm. I discussed this with Parks, who ultimately agreed to issue me an [apology letter](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1j8i7tb9FJ0tGO2j9ksG4oP0qMFJB0tHJ/view?usp=sharing). I appreciate the apology.  I hope Parks changes how they approach situations like this so other Tacoma residents do not have to go through what I have.

I sent the apology to the TNT and was pleasantly surprised to see they [wrote a story setting the record straight](https://www.thenewstribune.com/news/local/article317720825.html).

I am proud of my work repairing the Bayside Trails and the fact that the public is now legally allowed to walk one third of the trail in Garfield Gulch. I will continue to advocate to reopen the remaining two thirds of the trail, which is on City of Tacoma property and still remains closed.
