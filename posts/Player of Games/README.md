# Player of Games

I really enjoyed Player of Games by Ian Banks.  I read it perhaps 15 years ago while barreling through the entire culture series.  I am likely due for a re read.

A lot of the current zeitgeist focuses on singularity, though we've stopped using that term for some reason.  We speak mostly of annihiliation instead.  Regardless, the underlying concepts have to do with exponential takeoff and infinite loops.

30 years ago I played rather a lot of Magic the Gathering.  The game had flaws.  Certain cards broke the game.  Scarcity prevented issues.  Some enterprising players spent many thousands of dollars eliminating the scarcity issues and dominating the game.  The publisher then changed the rules.

I've been tinkering with cards from the initial releases.  Quite accidentally this is turning into a good way to think about exponential takeoff leveraging into post scarcity.  These two decks are perhaps illustrative:

* https://archidekt.com/decks/26595883/overpowered_atog
* https://archidekt.com/decks/26585501/overpowered_blue_verduran

The Atog decks abuses well know draw mechanics.  The Verduran deck is an updated version of the last deck I made circa 1998.  It abuses a more obscure draw mechanic.

In each case the decks don't play a normal balanced game of magic.  They are essentially a form of solitaire.  Sometime between 1 and 5 turns in they win the game.  Inspecting them superficially they seem potentially better than what is on the Vintage circuit today.  That's not entire surprising as they would cost 100s of thousands of dollars to construct.

Kurzweil has been saying we're in the takeoff trajectory for decades.  The funny part about exponents is they always look pretty flat when you're standing on them.  However, casting our mind's eye back to early transformers, deep learning or perhaps text and speech recognition work in the late 90s adds some perspective.

Things are changing fast.  It's pretty easy to imagine everything going post scarcity.  Storage has been for a while.  Compute would be but we keep cooking up use case for even more.  When this leaks properly into the real world everything changes.
