# Around the Corner

## AI

We're inching Zeno's paradox style closer and closer to AGI.  Maybe we're already there.  Regardless, we're so far up an exponential improvement curve that it's pretty hard to reason about timelines.  Is it now?  5 years?  10?  It's probably not 50.

The implications are broad.  Memorization serves no purpose.  Presumably memory is increasingly augmented by the artificial.  Reasoning as well.

In the extreme, it might exceed human capabilities broadly.  Ideally you end up with a Culture like outcome.

## Energy

We seem to finally be investing in fission again.  That may spur fusion investment.  Some proposals have powerplants with data centers.  Regardless, it seems like the US may finally invest in its hideously outdated power grid, forced by AI requirements.  The benefits would be broad.  Reduced energy cost benefit everyone.  They trickly down to cheap food, transport, etc.

## Menial Labor

One surprising outcome of AI so far is that humanoid robots are useful.  It turns out that they can be trained on the enormous corpus (har de har) of human knowledge.  There is an argument they may be more viable than purpose built robots for many tasks.

Imagine for a moment what might happen with an infinite amount of menial human labor available to you at some massively reduced cost, perhaps $1/hour.  Suddenly all our landscaping looks perfect.  We all end up like elder Rockefellers directing the robots to perfectly manicure our (much smaller) grounds.  Everyone has a perfectly edged golf course lawn.

House upkeep follows suite.  Painting becomes solely about cost of materials.  That bathroom grout job you've been putting off is no problem.

Want to restore a house covered in 100 years of paint?  No biggie.  Mr Roboto will hapilly scrub it away with a box of razor blades over 2000 hours with no complaints.

In real terms that means the value of existing land probably increases.  That is, unless everyone just moves to their own private estate.  You may have been told otherwise, but we have an awful lot of empty land.

Building cost presumably becomes cost of materials.  Caveat the masses would need to rise up and overthrow the existing permitting regime...

## Transport

Heavy lift drones can easily lift 100kg for $10k now.  The prices are rapidly dropping.  The poor mans option is a climbing harness and a rope from one of those.  It seems like it's going to become so cheap to do that everyone will "lawnchair larry" at some point.

FAA regulations prevent ultralights from flying in populated areas.  I wonder if that will change.
